Product Name
Voice of Customer Intelligence Platform

1. Product Vision
Build an AI-powered Voice of Customer Intelligence platform that transforms multilingual customer feedback into evidence-based marketing decisions.
Instead of simply analyzing reviews, the platform helps marketing teams understand customer experience, identify root causes behind customer dissatisfaction, benchmark competitors, validate business hypotheses, and generate actionable marketing recommendations.
The platform is initially designed for European ferry operators (e.g., DFDS, Stena Line, P&O Ferries), while remaining extensible to other industries.

2. Product Principles
2.1 Business-first, AI-second
The platform exists to support business decision making rather than simply demonstrating AI capabilities.
Every AI module should ultimately help marketing managers answer:
What happened? Why did it happen? What should we do next?

2.2 Evidence-based Insights
Every conclusion generated by AI must be supported by evidence.
Evidence may come from:
* Public customer reviews
* Survey responses
* Competitor comparison
* Customer journey analysis
The platform should avoid unsupported assumptions.

2.3 Explainable AI
Every AI-generated insight should include:
* Supporting evidence
* Confidence score
* Related customer feedback
* Reasoning process
Users should always understand why the AI reached a conclusion.

2.4 Multilingual by Default
Customer feedback may originate from multiple countries.
The platform should automatically detect languages and translate all customer feedback into English before AI analysis.
Original text should always remain accessible.

2.5 Global Data Consistency
Customer feedback collected from different platforms should be standardized before entering the AI reasoning pipeline.
Normalization includes:
* Language
* Currency
* Rating systems
* Date format
* Metadata

2.6 Actionable over Descriptive
The platform should prioritize business recommendations instead of only summarizing customer opinions.
The final output should always help users decide what actions should be taken.

3. Target Users
Primary users include:
* Marketing Managers
* Customer Experience (CX) Managers
* Customer Insight Analysts
* Marketing Analysts
* Product Managers
* Business Strategy Managers

4. Business Problems
Marketing teams currently collect customer feedback from multiple disconnected sources.
Examples include:
* Trustpilot
* Reddit
* Google Reviews
* App Store
* Google Play
* Internal customer surveys
These data sources differ in:
* Language
* Rating systems
* Currency
* Data quality
* Customer segments
As a result, customer insights are fragmented, difficult to compare, and difficult to transform into business decisions.

5. Product Goals
The platform should enable users to:
* Continuously collect public customer feedback
* Integrate optional survey datasets
* Standardize multilingual customer data
* Understand customer sentiment and pain points
* Identify root causes behind customer dissatisfaction
* Compare competitors
* Validate business hypotheses
* Generate evidence-based marketing recommendations
* Deliver executive-ready dashboards and reports

7. Knowledge Architecture

7.1 Knowledge Lifecycle
The platform is designed as a continuously learning marketing intelligence system rather than a one-time analytics tool.
Instead of generating isolated answers for every request, the platform should continuously accumulate organizational knowledge through the following lifecycle:

Retrieve Evidence
        ↓
Reason over Evidence
        ↓
Generate Business Insight
        ↓
Store Business Insight
        ↓
Retrieve Historical Insight
        ↓
Generate Better Future Insights

This continuous learning process enables the platform to improve reasoning quality, identify recurring business patterns, and build long-term organizational knowledge over time.

7.2 Three-layer Memory Architecture
The platform shall maintain three complementary memory layers that work together across the dashboard, Mia, and the supporting database.

Layer 1 — Short-term Memory
Short-term memory stores the immediate working context for the current chat turn or page session.
Examples include:
* Current page state
* Open route focus
* Recent chat turns
* Temporary upload queue state
* Active filters and UI selections

7.2.1 Purpose
Short-term memory helps Mia stay aligned with what the manager is looking at right now.

Layer 2 — Medium-term Memory
Medium-term memory stores active workstream context that should survive for the current project slice, batch, or review cycle.
Examples include:
* Upload batches in progress
* Passenger-profile slices currently under review
* Survey uploads awaiting validation
* Version lineage for the latest files
* Human feedback not yet rolled into long-term policy

7.2.2 Purpose
Medium-term memory keeps the active workflow coherent while the operator is still working on it.

Layer 3 — Long-term Memory
Long-term memory stores durable project knowledge that should be reused across sessions.
Examples include:
* Validated business insights
* Answering policies
* Platform architecture rules
* Reusable analysis patterns
* Approved passenger-profile and IT-flow assumptions
* Stable recommendations and explanations

7.2.3 Purpose
Long-term memory supports organizational learning, cross-session reuse, and consistent answers from Mia.

7.3 Evidence and Insight Repositories
The platform still needs structured repositories for evidence and insight, but they should now be understood as part of the longer memory layer rather than the whole memory model.

Evidence Repository
Stores raw or lightly normalized evidence collected from public and internal sources.

Insight Repository
Stores AI-generated business knowledge derived from previous analyses.

These repositories should remain traceable, versioned, and explainable.

7.4 Retrieval Strategy
Before every AI reasoning task, the platform shall retrieve the most relevant information from:
* Short-term memory for the current session state
* Medium-term memory for active batch or slice context
* Long-term memory for durable knowledge and policies
* Evidence repository for source-backed proof
* Insight repository for previous conclusions

7.5 Explainable AI Requirement
Every AI-generated conclusion shall be traceable back to supporting evidence and the relevant memory layer.
The platform should always be able to answer:
* What evidence supports this conclusion?
* Which memory layer informed the answer?
* Has this issue occurred before?
* How does this compare with historical analyses?


8. Core AI Modules

Module 1 — Public Data Collector
Collect customer feedback from multiple public sources.
Supported sources include:
* Trustpilot
* Reddit
* Google Reviews
* Apple App Store
* Google Play Reviews
* Company News
* Uploaded Survey CSV
The system should support continuous data collection.

Module 2 — Global Data Normalization Engine
Standardize all collected customer feedback into a unified analytical format before AI analysis.
Responsibilities include:
Language Standardization
* Detect review language
* Translate all reviews into English
* Preserve original text
Currency Standardization
* Detect currencies
* Convert all monetary values into DKK
* Preserve original values
Rating Standardization
Normalize different rating systems into a unified 0–100 score.
Examples:
* 1–5 Stars
* 1–10 Ratings
* NPS
* CSAT
Date Standardization
Convert all timestamps into ISO-8601 format.
Metadata Enrichment
Automatically identify:
* Country
* Language
* Platform
* Company
* Route
* Review Date
* Customer Segment (if available)

Module 3 — Customer Insight Engine
Extract customer insights including:
* Topics
* Sentiment
* Emotion
* Customer Journey Stage
* Customer Pain Points
* Emerging Trends

Module 4 — Root Cause Analysis Engine
Identify the underlying business reasons behind customer dissatisfaction.
Instead of summarizing reviews, the module should determine:
* Primary Root Causes
* Supporting Evidence
* Customer Journey Stage
* Business Impact
* Confidence Score
The engine should answer:
Why is customer satisfaction changing?

Module 5 — Competitor Benchmark Engine
Compare customer perception across competitors.
Examples include:
* DFDS
* Stena Line
* P&O Ferries
* Irish Ferries
Benchmark dimensions include:
* Customer Satisfaction
* Topics
* Pain Points
* Customer Sentiment
* Emotional Distribution
* Industry Average
The engine should identify:
* Relative Strengths
* Relative Weaknesses
* Competitive Opportunities

Module 6 — Hypothesis Validation Engine
Validate marketing assumptions using evidence from multiple data sources.
Example:
Customers mainly complain about ticket prices.
The engine should determine whether the hypothesis is:
* Supported
* Rejected
* Partially Supported
All conclusions must include supporting evidence.

Module 7 — Marketing Recommendation Engine
Generate prioritized business recommendations.
Each recommendation should include:
* Priority
* Business Impact
* Expected Improvement
* Supporting Evidence
* Reasoning
Recommendations should be directly linked to identified root causes.

Module 8 — Dashboard & Reporting Engine
Generate executive dashboards.
Support:
* Interactive Dashboard
* Executive Summary
* Downloadable Reports
The dashboard should focus on actionable business insights.

9. Expected User Experience
After completing data collection, users should immediately understand that customer feedback has been standardized.
The platform should display a Data Standardization Summary, including:
* 🌍 Languages Automatically Detected
* 🌐 Reviews Translated into English
* 💱 Currencies Normalized to DKK
* ⭐ Ratings Standardized to a Unified 0–100 Scale
* 📅 Dates Converted into a Unified Format
Users should also be able to switch between the translated English version and the original review text when needed.

10. Non-functional Requirements
The platform should be:
* Modular
* Scalable
* Explainable
* Extensible
* Maintainable
All AI modules should consume the Unified Customer Dataset instead of raw platform-specific data.

11. Future Roadmap
Although the MVP targets European ferry operators, the platform architecture should support future expansion into:
* Hotels
* Airlines
* Electric Vehicles
* Retail
* E-commerce
* Financial Services
without major redesign.
